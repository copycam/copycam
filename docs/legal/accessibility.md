# Accessibility statement

_Last updated: 23 October 2026_

I want everyone to be able to use CopyCam, including people who use a screen reader, a keyboard, magnification or other assistive technology.

## The standard

This website aims to meet Israeli Standard 5568, which is based on the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 at level AA.

## What's in place

- Every page works with a keyboard alone. Links and buttons are reached with Tab and show a visible focus outline.
- Text contrast meets the AA ratios: at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text.
- Pages have a clear heading structure, a declared language and descriptive titles.
- Pages fit phone screens and stay usable when zoomed to 200%, without scrolling sideways.
- Decorative graphics are hidden from screen readers, and the QR codes (in the demo, and on the computer page at copycam.app/app) are labelled.
- On the computer page, the text your phone sends is listed as ordinary text with a Copy button, and screen readers announce when it arrives and when it is copied.
- Nothing flashes.
- The privacy, terms, licence and accessibility pages follow your device's light or dark setting.

## How it was tested

I checked by hand in Chrome, on a computer and in phone view: keyboard navigation, focus outlines, contrast and zoom. The site has not yet been tested thoroughly with screen readers such as NVDA or VoiceOver.

## Known limitations

- The website's content is in English only, apart from the Hebrew version of this statement.
- In the interactive demo, picking words by dragging across the photo needs a mouse or a touch screen. With a keyboard, use All and Clear to choose what to send, then Send.
- The demo imitates a phone screen. The writing on its pictured router label is part of that picture.
- The CopyCam phone app (copycam.app/p, or the page the desktop companion serves to your phone) also picks words by dragging. Its Edit button lets you type or correct the text instead.
- Linking a phone means scanning a QR code with its camera. There is no way yet to link by typing a code.
- Sites I link to, such as GitHub and Cloudflare, have their own accessibility arrangements.
